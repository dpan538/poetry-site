About

Dai Pan 潘岱

Dai Pan (潘岱) is a writer and visual artist from China, based in Brisbane, Australia. He writes in English. His poems begin with images and follow what changes between places and languages.

Working across photography, printmaking, book design, and writing, Pan uses framing, repetition, and sequence to shape how a place is perceived. An image can change meaning when it returns in a poem. A photograph can shift in much the same way when it sits beside another image. Moving a photograph from a screen to a printed page can change how it is read, too. He works with colour, surface, and pacing to hold those shifts open long enough for readers to notice them.

Pan studied fine arts at the School of Visual Arts in New York, where he earned a BFA in Design. He now pursues a Master of Information Technology at the University of Queensland in Brisbane. His design research draws on computing and semantic analysis to trace changes in language over time and to examine how the structure of an archive shapes what its readers can discover.

Selected Research

  • Words Over Time traces changes in the frequency and meaning of selected words through historical sources.
  • AusFigures brings together Australian public texts about supernatural figures and makes their sources available for comparison.
  • MGD Archive brings records from multiple institutional collections into a rights-aware archive for graphic design history.
