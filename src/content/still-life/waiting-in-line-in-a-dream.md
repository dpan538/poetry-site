A twenty-two-year-old man waiting in line in a dream should not, first of all, ask himself where the line leads. That is a question an awake person would ask. A dream is not responsible for entrances, exits, windows, or staff.

Often the line is only a line, like a folded sock. Everyone stands inside it, close to the back of someone else’s head, and behind him there seems to be a stomach pressed against his back.

His feet are stepping on something, though he feels no liquid and no clear temperature, perhaps because it is close to body heat and cannot be noticed. The world is flowing, and people are less refined than the future we imagined. Especially when a twenty-two-year-old man lowers his head in a dream and sees his shoelaces, his fingers, the hem of his coat, and the back of a stranger’s head in front of him, he finds that life was never designed in advance.

The dream has no strange spectacle either.

The first way to pass the boredom is to watch the shoulders of the person in front.

Do not watch the face.

In dreams a face easily turns into a relative, a teacher, a passerby, or something already dead, already forgotten.

Shoulders are safer.

They rarely carry any unsettling smell, and may even be a little clean.

Propped up by a hanger, or sprayed with perfume.
