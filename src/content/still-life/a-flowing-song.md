The world is flowing, and people are less refined than the future we imagined.
Time is still, even if the heartbeat, they say,
is proof of something living.
Unavoidably, a kind of dream.

Riding wings that beat with the light of night,
alternating lamps flatten my sight,
and I bear the weight of the earth, our mother.
I stand in line inside a dream I made myself, and wake again and again,
as if this were not the world I live in.

Names are written on the map,
a point among points of a place once known,
made normal by the checking of the text wrap.
Someone wrote a name down, which means it will be forgotten.
A branch is cut, so the root has already been forgotten,
or who would be worthy, who would dare to look down on a giant,
and keep cutting at its fingers?

Those worlds that startled me wanted to catch the wild man and study him,
to roast the beast and eat it.
I watched them search one tree hollow after another.
The hollow broke off there.
Rain and people were both tangled in the moon.
I don’t understand,
for whose sake do I accept this,
letting a confusion that never basks in daylight
come to introduce the pain.

Some good matter has already flowed away from me.
Like a nightingale on a branch, whose scream can be read as song,
from far to near, brain and heart pulled apart by the sound,
and reality grows in the space between near and far,
and everything seems full of questions.
I lift my head and look at my own face on the wall. I have many questions.
Back then I was still studying art in New York,
doing the so-called research on wild men, or on spirited human forms
and the human wilderness,

In the sense of sociology and linguistics.

My teacher seemed unwilling to ask much,
looked at those arranged
or
unarranged words and stopped, with no
questions.

I said often
I dream of wild men.
they queue to drink water and take medicine,
even inside my dream.

Thinking back, things, I asked because I wanted to.

After all the questions have happened, without a quote,
it is hard for any fact to be fixed.

The teacher was surprised too, by the so-called problem
of sitting with a wild man beneath a white tree.
Who cares whose tree!

If what is lost is emotion and not inclination,
could it also be seen as a
strong, hallucinated indulgence:
We sat in a small office near the tea room,
frosted glass on all four sides.
I could see only the low part below, feet running past through the glass, stepping on an eighties wooden floor, creaking.

I watched the people on the street, all walking indifferently.
They looked at roads that stretched with their steps.
Smoke and fire were planted in their lungs,
and when a mouth opened, it drifted low there.
The throat made strange sounds, names, baffling ones.
They call, by what they are called.
